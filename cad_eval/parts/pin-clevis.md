# Pin and clevis

**Purpose:** a clevis joint: a tongue swinging between the two cheeks of a clevis on a pin, as on a linkage or a gate's latch. Three parts, printed separately and assembled.

**The files:** the clevis as `clevis.step`, the tongue as `tongue.step`, the pin as `pin.step`, each placed as assembled. The pin runs along Y.

**Size:**
- **Clevis:** a base 16 mm along X, 18 mm along Y, 6 mm tall, from Z = 0 to 6. Two cheeks stand on it, each 16 mm along X and 4 mm thick, from Y = -9 to -5 and from Y = 5 to 9, up to Z = 26. Each has a hole for the pin through it along Y, 6.3 mm across, centred at X = 0, Z = 18.
- **Tongue:** a bar 12 mm along X (X = -6 to 6) and 9.2 mm thick, between the cheeks with 0.4 mm on each side (Y = -4.6 to 4.6), from Z = 10 to 40. It has the same 6.3 mm hole along Y at Z = 18.
- **Pin:** 6 mm across, along Y from Y = -10 to 10, on the holes' axis (X = 0, Z = 18), with a head 10 mm across and 2 mm long from Y = -12 to -10.

**Tolerance:** ±0.05 mm on the pin's and the holes' diameters, so the pin's clearance in each hole is 0.1 to 0.2 mm; the tongue's gap to each cheek 0.3 to 0.5 mm; ±0.1 mm on everything else. No two parts may overlap.

**Placement:** as assembled, as above: the clevis centred on X = 0 and Y = 0 from Z = 0 to 26.
